A prompt is just what you type to the AI. But the difference between a lazy prompt and a good one is the difference between a shrug and a genuinely useful answer. The good news: there's a simple pattern, and you'll have it memorised by the end of this lesson.

## Meet the BRIEF method

Imagine briefing a smart new assistant who knows nothing about you. You'd naturally cover five things:

| Letter | Stands for | Ask yourself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B** | Background | What's the situation? Who's involved? |
| **R** | Role | Who should the AI act as? (an editor, a coach, a travel agent…) |
| **I** | Intent | What exactly do I want done, and why? |
| **E** | Examples | Is there a sample of what "good" looks like? |
| **F** | Format | How long? What structure? What tone? |

You won't need all five every time. But when an answer disappoints, one of these is almost always missing.

## Before and after

**Before:**

```prompt
Write a birthday message for my boss.
```

You'll get something generic and slightly cringeworthy.

**After:**

```prompt
Background: My manager, Priya, turns 50 on Friday. We're a small, informal design team. She's been very supportive this year, especially when I was ill in March.
Role: Act as a warm, witty card writer.
Intent: Write a short message for the team card from me personally.
Format: 2–3 sentences, warm but not over the top, one light joke about her legendary love of spreadsheets. No clichés like "another trip around the sun".
```

Same task, wildly better result, and it took 40 seconds longer to write.

## The three most powerful words: "because", "for" and "not"

- **"because"** gives the reason, which helps the AI make smart choices: *"Keep it short because she reads email on her phone."*
- **"for"** names the audience: *"Explain this for a nervous first-time home buyer."*
- **"not"** rules out what you don't want: *"Not salesy. No emojis."*

## Specific beats clever

There are no magic words. You don't need to say "you are a world-class genius". Clear, specific information beats tricks every time. If you'd need to tell a human helper something, tell the AI.

> [!TIP]
> Stuck on how to describe what you want? Ask the AI: *"I want to write a prompt for [task]. Ask me the questions you'd need answered to do this brilliantly."*

## Try it now: upgrade a lazy prompt

Take something you'd normally ask in five words. Rewrite it using BRIEF. Then send both versions in separate chats and compare. Do this three times this week and it'll become automatic.

```prompt
Here's a lazy prompt I'd normally write: "[your lazy prompt]". Rewrite it using Background, Role, Intent, Examples and Format so it gets a much better answer. Ask me for any details you need to fill in.
```

## Key takeaways

- BRIEF: **B**ackground, **R**ole, **I**ntent, **E**xamples, **F**ormat.
- Use "because", "for" and "not" to steer.
- Specific and clear beats clever and magical.
