Writing is where AI gives most people their first "wow". Not because it writes better than you, but because it removes the blank-page dread and the emotional friction of tricky messages.

## The 3-part message formula

For almost any message, give the AI three things:

1. **Who it's to** and your relationship.
2. **What you need** (the outcome, not just the topic).
3. **The tone** and any constraints.

```prompt
Write a message to my neighbour (friendly, we chat sometimes) asking them to keep their dog from barking early in the morning. I want to keep the relationship good. Tone: warm, a bit apologetic, not passive-aggressive. Under 80 words.
```

## Difficult replies without the drama

When a message upsets you, AI is a great buffer. Paste the message, vent honestly, then ask for a calm draft.

```prompt
Here's a message I received: [paste]. Honestly, I'm annoyed because [your reason]. I want to [outcome, e.g. keep the client but set a boundary]. Draft a calm, professional reply that's firm but kind. Then give me a shorter alternative.
```

> [!TIP]
> Never let AI auto-send emotional replies. Read the draft, sleep on it if needed, and edit it into your own words.

## Sound like yourself

Generic AI writing has a "voice": overly cheerful, a bit wordy, fond of words like "delve". Fix it by showing your style:

```prompt
Here are three emails I've written that sound like me: [paste]. Describe my writing style in 5 bullet points. Then use that style for all drafts in this chat.
```

Then add your style notes to your custom instructions so it sticks.

## Inbox triage

Paste a batch of emails (with personal details removed if needed) and ask:

```prompt
For each email below, give me: a one-line summary, whether it needs a reply (yes/no), urgency (today/this week/whenever), and a 1–2 sentence suggested reply where needed. Use a table.
```

Many email apps (Gmail, Outlook) now have AI built in to summarise threads and suggest replies, so try the built-in features too.

## Message types AI handles brilliantly

- Complaints and refund requests (firm, factual, with a clear ask)
- Saying no politely
- Following up when someone has gone quiet
- Thank-you notes and condolence messages (always personalise)
- Translating a message into another language, and checking the tone translated well

## Try it now

Find one message you've been putting off replying to. Use the difficult-replies prompt above. Edit the result, send it, and enjoy the relief.

## Key takeaways

- Give the AI the recipient, the outcome and the tone.
- Use it as an emotional buffer for tricky replies, but you press send.
- Teach it your writing style so drafts sound like you.
