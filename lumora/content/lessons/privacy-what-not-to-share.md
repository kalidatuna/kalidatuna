AI assistants are incredibly useful with context. But context means sharing information. The trick is knowing what's fine, what needs care, and what never goes in.

## The traffic-light system

**🟢 Green: share freely**
- General questions, public information, your own writing and ideas
- Recipes, travel plans, learning topics
- Anonymised situations ("a colleague keeps interrupting me in meetings")

**🟡 Amber: share with care (remove identifying details)**
- Documents like bills, contracts and letters: redact names, addresses, account numbers
- Health questions: describe symptoms without identifying details
- Work material: only in tools your employer approves
- Photos: check what's in the background (addresses, documents, faces)

**🔴 Red: never share**
- Passwords, PINs, security codes, recovery phrases
- Full card numbers, bank logins, ID or passport numbers
- Other people's private information without their permission
- Confidential work data in unapproved tools

## Settings worth checking (5 minutes)

In your assistant's settings, look for:

1. **Training opt-out:** whether your chats can be used to improve models.
2. **Memory:** what it remembers about you; view and delete entries.
3. **Chat history:** how long chats are kept; delete old ones you don't need.
4. **Temporary chats:** for one-off sensitive questions.
5. **Connected apps:** remove access you no longer use.

> [!NOTE]
> Business and enterprise plans usually have stronger privacy terms (for example, not training on your data by default). If you use AI for work, that's one more reason to use the approved business account.

## Redaction made easy

Before pasting a document, replace sensitive details with placeholders:

> "John Smith, 14 Elm Road, account 12345678" → "[NAME], [ADDRESS], account [NUMBER]"

The AI can still explain the document perfectly.

## Images and screenshots

Screenshots often contain more than you notice: notification banners, email addresses, open tabs, faces of others. Crop before you upload.

## Think about others

When asking for help with a conflict or a relationship, describe the situation without naming people or sharing their private messages verbatim. It's kinder, safer, and the advice is just as good.

## Key takeaways

- Green = general info; Amber = redact first; Red = never (passwords, card numbers, others' private data).
- Check training, memory, history and connection settings once, and revisit occasionally.
- Redact with placeholders and crop screenshots before sharing.
