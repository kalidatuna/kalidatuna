Here's a secret: experienced AI users rarely get perfect results on the first try. They get a decent draft, then **steer** it. A conversation is an editing session, not a vending machine.

## Why iteration beats a perfect prompt

Every message in a chat adds to the context. When you reply "shorter, and less formal", the AI keeps everything it already knows about your task. Starting over throws that away.

## Your follow-up toolkit

Keep these moves handy. They work on nearly anything.

| Move | Example follow-up |
| --- | --- |
| **Dial** | "Make it 30% shorter." / "Twice as detailed on step 3." |
| **Tone** | "Warmer." / "More confident, less apologetic." |
| **Audience** | "Rewrite this for a 10-year-old." / "...for a sceptical CFO." |
| **Options** | "Give me 3 alternative openings with different tones." |
| **Zoom** | "Expand only the second point." |
| **Keep/Kill** | "Keep the first paragraph exactly. Rewrite the rest." |
| **Critique** | "What's weak about this draft? Then fix it." |
| **Reason** | "Why did you suggest that? What are the trade-offs?" |

## The "pick and mix" technique

Ask for variety, then combine the best bits:

```prompt
Give me 5 different subject lines for this email: 2 playful, 2 direct, 1 curiosity-driven.
```

Then: *"Combine the urgency of #2 with the playfulness of #4."*

## When you *should* start fresh

- The chat has wandered across several topics.
- The AI keeps repeating a mistake despite corrections (the bad idea is "stuck" in context).
- You're starting a genuinely new task.

When you restart, carry over the good stuff: *"Here's the draft we ended up with: [paste]. Now help me with…"*

> [!TIP]
> Ask the AI to summarise the conversation into a reusable prompt: *"Write me a single prompt that would have produced this final version in one go."* Save that prompt. You've just built a template.

## Try it now

Take any short text (an email, a bio, a product description) and run it through three follow-ups in a row: a **dial**, a **tone** and a **critique**. Notice how quickly you converge on something you like.

## Key takeaways

- Treat chats as editing sessions. Small, specific follow-ups beat perfect first prompts.
- Use dial, tone, audience, options, zoom, keep/kill, critique and reason.
- Restart when the chat is muddled, and carry the good parts with you.
