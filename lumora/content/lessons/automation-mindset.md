Automation sounds technical, but the idea is simple: **when this happens, do that, automatically.** You already use automations: an out-of-office reply, a bill on direct debit, a phone alarm. AI makes automations far smarter because the "do that" can now include reading, writing and deciding.

## Triggers and actions

Every automation has two parts:

- **Trigger (when):** a new email arrives, a form is submitted, it's 8am on Monday, a file is added to a folder.
- **Actions (then):** save the attachment, add a row to a spreadsheet, send a message, and now *summarise it with AI*, *categorise it*, *draft a reply*.

```
When a new invoice email arrives  →  save the PDF to Drive  →  add a row to my "Bills" sheet  →  remind me 3 days before it's due
```

## The "worth automating?" test

Score a task from 1 to 5 on each:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| How **often** do I do it? | Frequent tasks pay back quickly |
| How **boring** is it? | Boring tasks are where mistakes and procrastination happen |
| How **rule-based** is it? | Clear rules are easy to automate |
| How **low-risk** is a mistake? | Start where errors are cheap to fix |

Tasks scoring 15+ are excellent candidates. Start with one.

## Great first automations for regular people

- Save email attachments (receipts, invoices) to a folder automatically.
- Get a daily summary of important emails sent to your phone.
- Log form responses (bookings, sign-ups, orders) to a spreadsheet and send a thank-you.
- A Monday morning AI briefing: your calendar, weather, and top 3 priorities.
- Track spending: forward receipts to an address and have AI extract amount, shop and category.

## The tools

You don't need to code. Popular no-code automation tools:

- **Zapier:** the easiest, with thousands of app connections and built-in AI steps.
- **Make:** visual and flexible, great value for more complex flows.
- **n8n:** powerful and can be self-hosted; popular with tinkerers.
- **Built-in automation:** Apple Shortcuts, Power Automate (Microsoft), Gmail filters, and scheduled tasks inside AI assistants themselves.

> [!TIP]
> Before automating, do the task manually once while writing down every step. That list *is* your automation design.

```prompt
Here's a list of things I do every week: [list]. Score each on frequency, boredom, rule-based-ness and low-risk (1–5). Rank them, and for the top 2 describe the trigger and actions for an automation, and which no-code tool would suit a beginner.
```

## Key takeaways

- Automation = trigger + actions. AI adds reading, writing and deciding.
- Start with frequent, boring, rule-based, low-risk tasks.
- Write down the manual steps first; that's your blueprint.
