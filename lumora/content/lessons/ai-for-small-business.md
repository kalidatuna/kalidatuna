You don't need an "AI strategy" or a consultant. Small businesses win with AI the same way they win at everything: by being practical. Find what's eating your time, fix it, measure it, repeat.

## Step 1: Find the bottlenecks

Spend one week noting tasks that are repetitive, slow or dreaded. Then ask AI to help prioritise:

```prompt
I run a [type of business] with [number] staff. Here are tasks that eat our time each week, with rough hours: [list]. Which 3 could AI or automation help with most, how, roughly how many hours might we save, and what's the risk if it goes wrong?
```

Common high-value areas:

| Area | Typical AI win |
| --- | --- |
| Customer enquiries | Draft replies, FAQ answers, booking confirmations |
| Marketing | Posts, newsletters, product descriptions, ad variations |
| Admin | Invoices, receipts, scheduling, meeting notes |
| Sales | Proposals, quotes, follow-up emails |
| Operations | Checklists, SOPs, training materials |

## Step 2: A lean AI toolkit

For most small businesses:

1. **One AI assistant** on a business plan (better privacy terms and admin controls than personal accounts).
2. **The AI already built into your tools:** email, accounting software, website builder, design tools.
3. **One automation tool** for repeated processes.

That's it. Resist subscribing to ten AI apps.

## Step 3: Write your "business brain"

Create one document the AI can use as context: what you sell, prices, policies, tone of voice, FAQs, who your customers are. Put it in a project, custom assistant or skill. Every output gets better instantly.

## Step 4: Ground rules for your team

A one-page AI policy prevents most problems:

- Which tools are approved.
- What must **never** be pasted in (customer personal data, passwords, financial details, confidential contracts).
- Who checks AI-written customer communication before it's sent.
- How to disclose AI use where appropriate.

```prompt
Draft a one-page, plain-English AI use policy for a [type] business with [number] staff. Cover approved tools, data we must not share, human review of customer-facing content, and disclosure. Friendly tone, not legalistic.
```

## Step 5: Measure

Before and after each change, note the time spent. "Replying to enquiries: 6 hours/week → 2 hours/week" is the kind of number that tells you where to invest next.

> [!NOTE]
> Laws on data protection, consumer rights and AI disclosure vary by country. If you handle customer data, check your obligations (for example, GDPR in the UK and EU).

## Key takeaways

- Find the bottlenecks first; fix one at a time and measure.
- Keep the toolkit lean: one assistant, built-in AI, one automation tool.
- Write a "business brain" document and a one-page AI policy.
