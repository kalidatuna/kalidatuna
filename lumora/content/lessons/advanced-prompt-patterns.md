Once BRIEF and follow-ups feel natural, these six patterns will take you from "good" to "how did you do that?". Each one is a reusable shape you can apply to hundreds of tasks.

## 1. Interview me first

When you're not sure what details matter, let the AI ask.

```prompt
I want help planning [goal]. Before you suggest anything, interview me. Ask one question at a time, up to 6 questions, then summarise what you learned and propose a plan.
```

## 2. Think it through step by step

For problems with several steps (budgets, schedules, logic puzzles, tricky decisions), ask the AI to work through it carefully. Many assistants have a dedicated "thinking" or "reasoning" mode that does this automatically; use it for hard problems.

```prompt
Work through this step by step and show your reasoning briefly before giving a final answer: [problem]
```

## 3. Critique, then improve

AI is often better at *spotting* problems than avoiding them the first time. Make it review its own work.

```prompt
Here's my draft: [paste]. First, list the 5 biggest weaknesses as a harsh but fair editor would. Then rewrite it fixing all of them.
```

## 4. The expert panel

Get multiple perspectives on a decision by asking the AI to argue different sides.

```prompt
I'm deciding whether to [decision]. Simulate a short discussion between a cautious financial planner, an enthusiastic career coach, and my practical best friend. Each gives their strongest point. Then summarise where they agree and what I should find out before deciding.
```

## 5. Compare options in a table

```prompt
Compare these options for [goal]: [A], [B], [C]. Use a table with rows for cost, time, effort, risks and best for. Then recommend one for someone who [your situation], and say what would change your recommendation.
```

## 6. Make it a checklist

Turn any process into something you can tick off, ideal for things you'll repeat.

```prompt
Turn this process into a step-by-step checklist with checkboxes. Flag steps that are easy to forget with ⚠️ and add a time estimate for each: [process]
```

> [!WARNING]
> "Step by step" makes answers more careful, but not infallible. For important numbers, check the maths or ask the assistant to use its calculator/code tool.

## Combine them

Real power comes from chaining: **interview me** → **compare options** → **critique** → **checklist**. That's essentially how consultants work, and now you can do it in ten minutes.

## Try it now

Pick a real decision you've been putting off. Run patterns 1, 5 and 4 in the same chat. Notice how much clearer you feel afterwards.

## Key takeaways

- Interview me, step by step, critique and improve, expert panel, compare, checklist.
- Use reasoning modes for genuinely hard problems.
- Chain patterns for big decisions and projects.
