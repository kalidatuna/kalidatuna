There are dozens of AI apps, but you only need one or two. The big assistants are now more alike than different, and the best one is usually the one you'll actually open every day.

## The main general-purpose assistants

| Assistant | Made by | Where it shines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ChatGPT** | OpenAI | All-rounder with a huge feature set: voice, images, research, custom GPTs, agents |
| **Claude** | Anthropic | Thoughtful writing, long documents, careful reasoning, Projects and Skills |
| **Gemini** | Google | Deep integration with Gmail, Docs, Drive, YouTube and Android |
| **Microsoft Copilot** | Microsoft | Built into Windows, Edge and Microsoft 365 (Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams) |
| **Meta AI** | Meta | Inside WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger; handy for quick questions |
| **Perplexity** | Perplexity | Answer engine with sources: great for quick research with citations |

> [!NOTE]
> AI products change monthly. Names, models and prices shift, but the *categories* in this lesson stay stable. Our Tool Finder is updated regularly.

## How to choose in 3 questions

**1. Where do you already live?**
If your life runs on Google (Gmail, Docs, Android), try Gemini first. If you're in Microsoft 365 at work, Copilot may already be paid for. On an iPhone, the major apps all work well, and some are built into the system.

**2. What will you do most?**
- Writing, editing and thinking through problems: Claude or ChatGPT.
- Quick researched answers with links: Perplexity, or any assistant's web search mode.
- Images, voice chat and an all-in-one toolkit: ChatGPT or Gemini.

**3. Free or paid?**
Every major assistant has a free tier that is genuinely good. Paid plans (typically around $20/month) mostly give you:

- Stronger models and "thinking" modes
- Much higher usage limits
- Bigger file uploads, deep research, agents and projects

**Our advice:** use the free versions of two assistants for two weeks. Upgrade the one you reach for most once you start hitting limits.

## A simple setup that works for most people

1. **One main assistant** on your phone and computer (install the app; pin it).
2. **One research tool** for questions where sources matter.
3. **The AI already inside your tools** (Gmail, Word, Notion, Canva) for in-place help.

## Side-by-side test (5 minutes)

Paste the same prompt into two assistants and compare:

```prompt
I have 30 minutes and these ingredients: eggs, spinach, half an onion, feta, rice from yesterday, and spices. Suggest two dinner options, with step-by-step instructions, and tell me which one is faster.
```

Which one felt more useful? Which style do you prefer? That's your answer. There's no wrong choice.

## Key takeaways

- The top assistants are all capable; pick based on where you already work and what you'll do most.
- Free tiers are great for learning. Upgrade when limits get in your way.
- A practical kit: one main assistant, one research tool, and the AI built into apps you already use.
