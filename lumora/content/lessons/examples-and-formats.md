Describing a style in words is hard. Showing an example is easy. And asking for the right *format* means you can paste the result straight into an email, spreadsheet or document without any tidying.

## Few-shot prompting: learning from examples

"Few-shot" just means giving a few examples of what you want. The AI picks up the pattern, including style, length and structure, far better than from a description.

```prompt
I write short, punchy product descriptions for my candle shop. Here are two I like:

1. "Cedar & Smoke. Like a cabin fire you don't have to tend. 45-hour burn."
2. "Sea Salt Fig. Summer evening, bottled. Soft, sweet, a little salty. 40-hour burn."

Write descriptions in exactly this style for: Lavender & Oat (50-hour burn), Black Cherry (35-hour burn), Rain on Pine (45-hour burn).
```

Two examples are usually enough. Three is plenty.

## Ask for the format you'll actually use

The AI can produce almost any structure. Just ask for it by name:

| You need… | Ask for… |
| --- | --- |
| Something to paste in a spreadsheet | "A table with columns: Date, Task, Owner, Status" or "CSV" |
| A to-do list | "A checklist with checkboxes, grouped by day" |
| A decision | "A pros/cons table, then a one-line recommendation" |
| A study aid | "10 flashcards: question on one line, answer on the next" |
| A quick read | "A TL;DR in one sentence, then 3 bullets" |
| A script | "A conversation script with my lines and likely responses" |

## Templates: fill-in-the-blank power

Ask the AI to create a reusable template, then reuse it forever:

```prompt
Create a reusable template I can fill in each week for a short update to my team. Include sections for: wins, priorities, blockers, and asks. Keep each section to 1–3 bullets. Show the empty template, then an example filled in for a marketing coordinator.
```

## Constraints are your friend

Constraints focus the output:

- **Length:** "under 100 words", "exactly 5 bullets"
- **Reading level:** "for a 12-year-old", "for an expert"
- **Must include / must avoid:** "mention the deadline", "no jargon"
- **Structure:** "start with the answer, then explain"

> [!TIP]
> If the output keeps drifting from your format, paste an empty skeleton of exactly what you want and say: *"Fill in this structure exactly. Don't add sections."*

## Try it now

Pick a weekly task you do in a spreadsheet or list. Ask the AI to produce it as a table with named columns and three realistic sample rows. Paste it into your spreadsheet app and see it land perfectly.

## Key takeaways

- One or two examples beat a paragraph of description.
- Name the format: table, checklist, CSV, flashcards, script.
- Build templates once and reuse them weekly.
