The newest wave of AI goes beyond answering. **Scheduled tasks** run prompts for you automatically, and **agents** can carry out multi-step jobs: browsing websites, filling forms, using apps and creating files. Here's how to use them well.

## Scheduled tasks: your AI on a timer

Many assistants now let you schedule a prompt to run daily or weekly and send you the result.

Ideas that work brilliantly:

```prompt
Every weekday at 7:30am: Give me a short briefing: today's weather for [city], my calendar for today, and one thing I should prepare for. Keep it under 120 words.
```

```prompt
Every Friday at 4pm: Search for news from this week about [your industry/interest] and give me the 5 most important stories with one sentence each and a link.
```

```prompt
On the 1st of each month: Remind me of my monthly admin checklist: [list], and ask what I've completed.
```

## Agents: AI that takes actions

An **agent** is an AI that can plan and carry out several steps towards a goal, using tools like a web browser, files, code or connected apps. Examples:

- "Find three highly rated plumbers near me who work Saturdays and put their details in a table."
- "Compare prices for this exact blender across these four shops."
- "Fill in this conference registration form using my details" (with your approval before submitting).

Agents might live in your assistant (an "agent mode"), in your browser, or in automation tools.

## Guardrails: stay in control

Agents are powerful but fallible, and they can be misled by what they read online. Use these rules:

1. **Approve before anything irreversible:** sending, buying, booking, deleting or posting.
2. **Never put passwords or card numbers into the chat.** If an agent needs to log in, use the app's secure takeover/login feature, or do that step yourself.
3. **Watch first runs.** Most agents show what they're doing; observe until you trust a task.
4. **Limit scope:** give clear goals, budgets and boundaries ("Don't spend more than $50", "Only use these three websites").
5. **Check the output:** agents can misread pages or stop halfway.

> [!WARNING]
> Be especially careful with agents that read your email or browse unfamiliar sites. Hidden "prompt injection" instructions can try to hijack them. Keep approvals on.

## Agents vs. automations

| | Automation (Zapier/Make/n8n) | Agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | The same steps every time | Tasks that need figuring out |
| Reliability | Very high once set up | Good, improving, needs supervision |
| Setup | You design each step | You describe the goal |

Many people use both: agents for one-off research and errands, automations for repeated processes.

## Key takeaways

- Scheduled tasks give you briefings, digests and reminders on autopilot.
- Agents can complete multi-step tasks, but need goals, limits and supervision.
- Always approve irreversible actions, and never share passwords or card numbers in chat.
