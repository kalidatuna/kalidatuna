The same tools that write your emails can write convincing scam messages, fake a video, or clone a voice from a short clip. You don't need to be paranoid, just prepared. A few habits defeat almost every AI-powered scam.

## The new scam toolkit

| Scam | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Voice clone calls** | A "family member" calls in distress needing money urgently |
| **Deepfake video** | A fake video of a celebrity, CEO or official endorsing an investment |
| **Perfect phishing** | Flawless, personalised emails from your "bank" or "delivery company" |
| **Fake support** | Chatbots or websites impersonating real companies |
| **Romance & friendship scams** | AI-assisted fake personas building trust over weeks |
| **Investment "AI bots"** | Promises of guaranteed returns from "AI trading" |

## The warning signs (they haven't changed)

AI makes scams more polished, but they still rely on the same pressure tactics:

- **Urgency:** "You must act now."
- **Secrecy:** "Don't tell anyone."
- **Unusual payment:** gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, "safe accounts".
- **Emotional pressure:** fear, panic, love, greed.
- **Too good to be true:** guaranteed returns, prizes, miracle deals.

Spelling mistakes are no longer a reliable sign. Judge the **request**, not the polish.

## Habits that defeat AI scams

1. **Hang up and call back** on a number you already know (not one given in the message).
2. **Set up a family safe word,** a word only your family knows, to verify emergencies by phone.
3. **Never pay or share codes** because of an unexpected call, text or email.
4. **Verify investment offers** with official financial regulators' warning lists.
5. **Slow down.** Legitimate organisations will wait while you check.

> [!TIP]
> Have a 10-minute conversation with older relatives about voice cloning and safe words. It's one of the most valuable things you can do with this lesson.

## Use AI to fight AI

AI can help you check suspicious messages:

```prompt
I received this message: [paste, with any personal details removed]. Does it show signs of a scam? What would a legitimate version look like, and how should I verify it safely?
```

Don't click links inside suspicious messages to "check" them. Go to the official website directly.

## If you've been scammed

- Contact your bank immediately; speed matters.
- Change passwords and turn on two-factor authentication.
- Report it to your national fraud reporting service and the platform involved.
- Tell people you trust. Scams are designed to make you feel ashamed; you're not alone.

## Key takeaways

- AI makes scams more convincing, but the pressure tactics are the same.
- Hang up and call back; set a family safe word; never pay under pressure.
- Use AI to analyse suspicious messages, without clicking their links.
