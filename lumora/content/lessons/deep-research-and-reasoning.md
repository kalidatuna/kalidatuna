Most assistants now offer extra-powerful modes. Two of the most useful are **reasoning** (sometimes called "thinking") and **deep research**. They're slower, but on the right problems they're dramatically better.

## Reasoning / thinking modes

These modes spend extra time working through a problem before answering: breaking it into steps, checking their work, considering alternatives.

**Use them for:**
- Multi-step planning (a complex trip, a project schedule, a budget with trade-offs)
- Maths, logic and data analysis
- Tricky decisions with many factors
- Debugging a spreadsheet or reviewing a contract's logic

**Don't bother for:** quick rewrites, simple questions or casual chat. A standard mode is faster.

```prompt
Think carefully about this. We're a family of 4 deciding between: (A) keeping our 9-year-old car and repairing it ($2,400 now, likely more later), or (B) buying a used hybrid ($18,000, $4,000 trade-in). We drive 12,000 miles a year; fuel costs ~$3.50/gallon. Compare 5-year total costs with clear assumptions, show the maths, and tell me which assumptions matter most.
```

## Deep research mode

Deep research runs many web searches, reads dozens of sources, and writes a structured, cited report. It can take several minutes, so go make a coffee.

**Use it for:**
- Big purchase research (heat pumps, cars, schools, software)
- Understanding a new field quickly
- Market or competitor research for a business
- Background before an important meeting or decision

## How to brief deep research

Deep research works best with a proper brief, like hiring a researcher:

```prompt
Research question: What are the realistic pros, cons and costs of installing a heat pump in a 1970s three-bedroom house in [country/region]?
Context: Moderately insulated, gas heating now, budget up to [amount], planning to stay 10+ years.
Please cover: upfront costs and available grants, running costs vs. gas, installation issues for older homes, noise, reliability, and questions to ask installers.
Prioritise official and independent sources from the last 2 years. Present a summary first, then details, then a checklist.
```

Many tools will ask clarifying questions first. Answer them carefully, as it makes a big difference.

## Reading the report like a pro

- Read the **summary**, then **spot-check 3 sources**: open them and confirm the claims.
- Ask follow-ups: "Which findings are most uncertain?", "What would change the conclusion?"
- Turn it into action: "Make this into a one-page decision memo and a list of next steps."

> [!TIP]
> Save great research briefs as templates in your library. A good brief is reusable across countless topics.

## Key takeaways

- Reasoning modes: multi-step, mathematical and tricky decisions.
- Deep research: big questions needing many sources; brief it like a researcher.
- Always spot-check sources and ask where the uncertainty lies.
