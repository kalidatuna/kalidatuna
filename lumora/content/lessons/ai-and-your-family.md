AI is already in your family's life, through homework, phones, smart speakers and apps. The goal isn't to ban it or ignore it, but to help everyone use it **wisely**.

## Kids and homework: tutor, not ghostwriter

The rule that works for most families and schools:

> **AI can explain, quiz and give feedback. It shouldn't do the work for you.**

Set up a tutor-style prompt together:

```prompt
You're a patient tutor for a [age]-year-old. Never give the final answer to homework questions. Instead, ask guiding questions, give hints, and explain concepts with simple examples. Check understanding with a quick question at the end. Be encouraging.
```

And always: **follow the school's rules** on AI use. Many schools now have clear policies; ask if you're unsure.

## Age and settings

- Most major AI services have **minimum ages** (often 13+, sometimes 18+, or with parental consent). Check the terms.
- Use **family or teen accounts** and parental controls where available.
- Keep devices in shared spaces for younger children.

## Talk about it (better than any setting)

Conversations to have with kids and teens:

- **"AI can be wrong."** Show them a hallucination together.
- **"AI isn't a person."** It can sound caring, but it doesn't actually know or love them.
- **"Some images and videos are fake."** Look at examples of deepfakes together.
- **"Never share private stuff."** Names, school, address, photos.
- **"Tell me if anything feels weird."** No punishment for asking.

## AI companions and emotional use

Some apps offer AI "friends" or companions. They can feel comforting, but for young people especially they can encourage isolation or unhealthy dependence. Watch for:

- Secretive or excessive use
- Preferring the AI to real friends
- Sharing emotional struggles only with an AI

If someone in your family is struggling emotionally, AI is not a substitute for real people and professional support.

## Older relatives

AI can be wonderful for older people: voice assistants, reading letters aloud, explaining technology, companionship through conversation, staying in touch. Help them by:

- Setting up voice mode and large text.
- Teaching the **scam habits** from the previous lesson, especially voice clones and safe words.
- Showing them how to ask follow-up questions ("What does that mean?").

## A family AI agreement

```prompt
Help us write a friendly one-page family agreement about using AI. Our family: [ages]. Cover homework, privacy, what to do if something seems wrong, screen time, and checking facts. Make it positive, not a list of punishments.
```

## Key takeaways

- Tutor, not ghostwriter: AI should help kids learn, within school rules.
- Check age limits and settings, but conversations matter most.
- Watch for unhealthy companion-app use; help older relatives with voice features and scam awareness.
