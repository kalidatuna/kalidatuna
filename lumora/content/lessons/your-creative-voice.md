The biggest risk with creative AI isn't that it's bad. It's that it's *average*. Left alone, AI produces the most likely, middle-of-the-road output. Your taste, opinions and experiences are what make work worth reading, watching or buying.

## AI as a partner, not a replacement

Healthy creative roles for AI:

- **Brainstorm partner:** "Give me 30 wildly different angles for a story about a lighthouse keeper." (Then pick the weird one.)
- **Sparring partner:** "What's clichéd about this opening? Be blunt."
- **Production assistant:** formatting, resizing, captions, variations, tidying.
- **Research assistant:** "What did 1920s Paris cafés actually serve?"

Roles to keep for yourself: **the idea, the point of view, the final choices.**

## Feed it *you*

```prompt
Here are three pieces of my writing [paste]. Analyse what makes my voice distinctive: sentence rhythm, word choices, humour, themes. When you help me draft, preserve these traits and point out where a suggestion of yours sounds generic.
```

Then edit hard. A good rule: **if you wouldn't defend a sentence, delete it.**

## Escape the average

- Ask for **more options** than you need, then choose the strangest good one.
- Add **constraints:** "No metaphors about journeys." "Every line must mention a smell."
- Use **specifics from your life:** names, places, memories. AI can't invent your experiences.

## Copyright, credit and fairness

This area is evolving and varies by country, so treat this as general guidance, not legal advice:

- **Check each tool's terms** on ownership and commercial use of what you create.
- **Purely AI-generated work may not be protected by copyright** in some places, while work with substantial human creative input is more likely to be. Keep your drafts as a record of your contribution.
- **Avoid imitating living artists by name**, especially for commercial work. It's widely seen as unfair and can carry legal risk. Develop your own style instead.
- **Disclose AI use** where your audience, client, school or publisher expects it.

> [!TIP]
> Keep a "taste file": a folder of work you love and notes on *why*. Use it to brief AI and to remind yourself what you're aiming for.

## Try it now

Take something creative you've made. Ask AI for a blunt critique and 10 directions it could go. Choose one, then make the next version mostly by hand, using AI only for the parts you find tedious.

## Key takeaways

- AI defaults to average; your taste and specifics make the work.
- Use AI for brainstorming, critique and production, and keep the ideas and choices yours.
- Check terms, respect other creators, and disclose AI use where expected.
