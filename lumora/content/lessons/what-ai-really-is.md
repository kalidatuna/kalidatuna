You've heard that AI will change everything. You've also heard it's overhyped. Both are a little bit true, and neither helps you decide what to *do* on a Tuesday afternoon. This lesson gives you a clear, practical mental model you'll use for the rest of the course.

When most people say "AI" today, they mean **AI assistants** like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot: apps you can talk to in plain language, which write, explain, summarise, brainstorm, analyse files and images, and increasingly take actions for you.

## The one-sentence version

An AI assistant is **a very well-read, very fast, occasionally overconfident helper** that has absorbed patterns from an enormous amount of text and can use those patterns to produce useful language on demand.

Hold onto three words from that sentence:

- **Well-read.** It has seen more writing than any human could read in a thousand lifetimes, so it knows a little about almost everything.
- **Fast.** A first draft that would take you an hour takes it ten seconds.
- **Overconfident.** It doesn't "know" when it's wrong in the way you do. It can state a made-up fact in the same calm tone as a real one.

## What AI assistants are great at

| Great at | Example |
| --- | --- |
| First drafts | "Write a polite reminder to a client about an overdue invoice." |
| Rewriting & tone | "Make this message warmer and half as long." |
| Explaining | "Explain inflation like I'm 12, then like I'm an economics student." |
| Summarising | "Summarise this 30-page PDF into 5 bullet points and 3 open questions." |
| Brainstorming | "Give me 20 birthday party ideas for a 7-year-old who loves space." |
| Structuring | "Turn these messy notes into a checklist with deadlines." |
| Reading images & files | "What does this error message on my screen mean?" |

## Where they stumble

- **Precise facts and figures.** Dates, statistics, quotes and citations can be invented (this is called a *hallucination*).
- **Very recent events,** unless the assistant is connected to web search.
- **Your private context.** It doesn't know your boss, your budget or your dog's name unless you tell it.
- **Judgement calls with real consequences.** Medical, legal and financial decisions need a qualified human.

> [!TIP]
> A useful rule: **AI drafts, you decide.** Let it do the heavy lifting on words and ideas; keep your hand on facts, money, and anything you'll sign your name to.

## AI is not magic, and not a person

It's tempting to treat an assistant like a search engine (type keywords, get facts) or like a person (it has feelings, it remembers you). Neither is quite right.

It's closer to a **brilliant intern on their first day**: capable, eager, knowledgeable, but with zero context about your life. The better you brief them, the better the work. That idea, *briefing*, is the heart of the Prompting module.

## Try it now

Open any AI assistant (free versions are fine) and paste this:

```prompt
I'm completely new to AI assistants. In 5 short bullet points, tell me the most useful things you can help with in an ordinary person's week. Then ask me 2 questions about my life so you can suggest something specific I could try today.
```

Answer its two questions. Notice how much better the second answer is once it knows a bit about you. That's the whole game.

## Key takeaways

- An AI assistant predicts useful text from patterns. It's fluent, fast and well-read, but it isn't infallible.
- Use it for drafts, explanations, summaries, ideas and structure.
- Verify facts, figures and anything high-stakes.
- Context is everything: the more it knows about your situation, the more useful it becomes.
