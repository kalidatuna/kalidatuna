Productivity experts agree: a weekly review is the highest-leverage habit there is. AI makes it fast and, surprisingly, enjoyable.

## Before you start (2 minutes)

Gather: next week's calendar (or connect it to your assistant), your task list, and your goals for the month. Open a dedicated project or chat called *Weekly planning* so the AI remembers past weeks.

## Part 1: Look back (5 minutes)

```prompt
Weekly review time. Here's what I planned last week: [paste or "see previous chat"]. Here's what actually happened: [quick notes]. Ask me 3 short reflection questions, one at a time: what went well, what got in the way, and what I want to change. Then summarise one lesson for next week.
```

## Part 2: Plan forward (10 minutes)

```prompt
Here's my calendar for next week: [paste or use connector]. My task list: [paste]. My goals this month: [goals]. My energy pattern: [e.g. best focus in the mornings].
1. Pick my top 3 priorities for the week and explain why.
2. Suggest time blocks for them in my calendar's free space, with buffers.
3. Flag overloaded days and suggest what to move, delegate or drop.
4. List quick tasks I could batch into one 45-minute admin block.
Be realistic. Plan for 70% of my free time, not 100%.
```

## Part 3: Commit (3 minutes)

- Put the time blocks in your calendar.
- Write the top 3 priorities somewhere visible.
- Tell the AI: "Remind me of these 3 priorities when I check in on Wednesday."

## Mid-week check-in (3 minutes, Wednesday)

```prompt
Mid-week check-in. Here's my progress on the top 3: [notes]. What should I adjust for Thursday and Friday so I finish the most important things?
```

## Automate it (optional)

If your assistant supports scheduled tasks, schedule a Sunday 6pm message: "It's planning time. Here's your calendar for next week and last week's priorities. Ready for your 3 reflection questions?" Now the ritual starts itself.

> [!TIP]
> Keep every weekly plan in the same project. After a month, ask: "Looking at my last four weeks, what patterns do you see in what I plan versus what I do?" The insights are often eye-opening.
